## KUDO at a glance

KUDO is a language-access platform for meetings, webinars, events, and venues. It has two deliberately different live paths: KUDO AI Speech Translator and professional human interpretation. The official site says the AI service provides live audio and captions in 70-plus languages, while its human interpreter service covers more than 200 spoken and sign languages. These are different products and coverage statements, so a planning document should not combine figures or assume a language available through human interpretation is also available as AI audio.

For an in-person event, KUDO describes an attendee journey built around a QR code or web link: participants open the selector on a mobile device, choose a language, then use headphones for translated audio or read subtitles. Its online-platform page says the product can be used through KUDO’s meeting platform or an embedded language-selector widget. It names Teams, Zoom, Google Meet, ON24, Bizzabo, RingCentral, Hubilo, EventMobi, and GlobalMeet Webcast among presented paths. Verify the integration, account tier, audio handoff, and attendee limit before a show; a logo list is not a tested design for every event.

## Audio, captions, and interpretation

KUDO AI is native live functionality because it translates an active source into audience-facing outputs while the session runs. The vendor calls it continuous, live speech translation. In presentation mode, it offers translated audio plus captions; in conversational mode, its page says it offers ultra-fast translated captions only. The online platform page also calls the AI mode one-way communication. These constraints matter: a translated-caption conversation is not a multi-party interpreted-audio discussion, and neither is automatically a dubbed program recording.

The human route has another interaction model. KUDO says professional interpretation supports one-way and interactive communication and can be selected alongside AI. That can be appropriate where a discussion needs language mediation, but interpreter scheduling, subject matter, languages, and confidentiality still need a real plan. The official material says 12,000 interpreters are available on demand; that does not establish availability for a particular specialist, language pair, or time zone.

KUDO lists custom glossary upload, custom voices by gender, and automatic language detection for AI Speech Translator. Treat these as configuration features, not evidence that multilingual content will be accurate under every acoustic condition. Test names, numbers, acronyms, code-switching, overlapping talk, and regional variants. In a venue, test the audio-to-USB interface and internet path. For an online event, test the widget in the actual platform, browser, mobile device, and access-control setup.

## Commercial and technical scope

Public product pages do not show a general numeric price table. KUDO’s reseller material refers to consumption-based pricing, and a government page describes an annual license per room, but neither supports a universal end-user quote. Pricing is sales-led and free access is unknown. Reviewed official pages show widgets and integrations but do not establish a public developer API, so API availability is unknown rather than inferred.

Choose KUDO when a meeting or event requires live AI translated audio and captions, with a separate route to human interpreting. It fits presentation-style communication especially well in the documented AI mode. It is not evidenced as a post-production dubbing service, and captions-only conversational mode should never be advertised as translated audio. Compare Wordly and Interprefy for adjacent event-language paths, and SyncWords or AI-Media LEXI for caption- and broadcast-centric outputs. Document who owns speaker preparation, technical rehearsal, attendee instructions, and live escalation before doors open.
